<p dir="auto">From the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_equations" rel="nofollow">wikipedia article on the Fresnel Equations</a>:
"The Fresnel equations (or Fresnel coefficients) describe the reflection and transmission of light (or electromagnetic radiation in general) when incident on an interface between different optical media."</p>
<p dir="auto">This package defines 8 functions that implement the equations found in the wikipedia article. An overview of the functions is given below.</p>

<p dir="auto">Note that the transmittance <code>T</code> could be defined as <code>1 - R</code>, guaranteeing perfect energy conservation. This implementation can however suffer <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catastrophic_cancellation" rel="nofollow">catastrophic cancellation</a> as <code>R</code> approaches 1. Instead, the transmission coefficient <code>t</code> is used directly, meaning that conservation of energy is only accurate to numerical precision.</p>

<p dir="auto">Note that some assumptions are made in deriving these equations:</p>
<ol dir="auto">
<li>The interface between the media is flat</li>
<li>The media are homogeneous and isotropic</li>
<li>The media are non-magnetic, i.e. with a <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permeability_(electromagnetism)" rel="nofollow">permeability</a> equal to that of vacuum.</li>
</ol>
