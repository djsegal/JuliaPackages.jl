<p dir="auto">CoordinateConverterGK.jl is a Julia package for conversion between the Cartesian coordinates and the geographic coordinates for a point with the Gauss-Krüger Projection. The formulae are based on documents and publications of <a href="https://www.gsi.go.jp/ENGLISH/index.html" rel="nofollow">the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan</a> (see <strong>References</strong>).</p>

<p dir="auto">Given a central meridian <code>λ₀</code> and latitude of origin <code>φ₀</code> (in degrees), Cartesian coordinates can be converted with the following code:</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="using CoordinateConverterGK
λ, φ = xy2lonlat(λ₀, φ₀, easting, northing)"><pre><span class="pl-k">using</span> CoordinateConverterGK
λ, φ <span class="pl-k">=</span> <span class="pl-c1">xy2lonlat</span>(λ₀, φ₀, easting, northing)</pre></div>
<p dir="auto">where <code>λ</code> and <code>φ</code> are the converted longitude and latitude, <code>easting</code> and <code>northing</code> are the eastward and northward distances (in meters) from the origin, respectively.
A function <code>yx2latlon</code> is available in the same manner:</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="φ, λ = yx2latlon(φ₀, λ₀, northing, easting)"><pre>φ, λ <span class="pl-k">=</span> <span class="pl-c1">yx2latlon</span>(φ₀, λ₀, northing, easting)</pre></div>
<p dir="auto">Similarly, functions <code>lonlat2xy</code> and <code>latlon2yx</code> can convert geographic coordinates to Cartesian coordinates for a point.</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="x, y = lonlat2xy(λ₀, φ₀, λ, φ)"><pre>x, y <span class="pl-k">=</span> <span class="pl-c1">lonlat2xy</span>(λ₀, φ₀, λ, φ)</pre></div>

<p dir="auto">When the Japan Plane Rectangular Coordinate System is adopted,
functions such as <code>xy2lonlat_ja</code> and <code>lonlat2xy_ja</code> can omit the coordinates of origin <code>λ₀</code>, <code>φ₀</code>.
These functions require the zone number of interest (1 to 19) instead.</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="λ, φ = xy2lonlat_ja(9, easting, northing) # in case of zone IX"><pre>λ, φ <span class="pl-k">=</span> <span class="pl-c1">xy2lonlat_ja</span>(<span class="pl-c1">9</span>, easting, northing) <span class="pl-c"><span class="pl-c">#</span> in case of zone IX</span></pre></div>
