<p dir="auto">PubChem.jl is a powerful Julia package that facilitates easy access to chemical data from the PubChem database and associates it with chemical species defined using the Catalyst.jl package. This allows you to seamlessly integrate chemical metadata into your computational workflows.</p>

<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="using PubChem, Catalyst

<span class="pl-c1">@attach_metadata</span> H2O</pre></div>

<p dir="auto">With metadata attached to species directly, you can now utilize these chemical properties to perform various calculations, such as determining the limiting reactant and calculating theoretical yields in a balanced Catalyst reaction.</p>
<p dir="auto">As an example, let's consider the reaction <code>2Al + 3Cl2 --&gt; 2AlCl3</code> and suppose we have the masses of <code>Al</code> and <code>Cl2</code> as 2.80g and 4.15g, respectively.</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="# Define the species involved in the reaction
@variables t
@species Al(t), Cl2(t), AlCl3(t)

# Attach metadata to the species
@attach_metadata Al 
@attach_metadata Cl2 
@attach_metadata AlCl3 

# Define a balanced Catalyst reaction 
rx = Reaction(1.0, [Al, Cl2], [AlCl3], [2, 3], [2])

# Get limiting reagent given the masses of the reactants
julia&gt; limiting_reagent(rx,[2.80,4.15])
(Cl2(t), 0.05853314527503526) # Returns the limiting reagent and it's number of moles

# Calculate theoretical yield given the masses of the reactants and the product for which to calculate
julia&gt; theoretical_yield(rx,[2.80,4.15],AlCl3)
