<p dir="auto"><strong>ComputationAwareKalman.jl</strong> implements the <em>computation-aware Kalman filter</em> (CAKF) and the <em>computation-aware RTS smoother</em> (CAKS), novel approximate, probabilistic numerical versions of the Kalman filter and RTS smoother that are</p>
<ol dir="auto">
<li><em>matrix-free</em> and <em>iterative</em>, and can fully leverage modern parallel hardware (i.e. GPUs);</li>
<li><em>more efficient</em> than their standard versions, with <strong>quadratic time</strong> (worst-case) and <strong>linear memory</strong> complexities; and</li>
<li>computation-aware, i.e. they come with theoretical guarantees for their uncertainty estimates which capture the inevitable approximation error.</li>
</ol>
<p dir="auto">In our paper we have demonstrated the scalability of the approach by applying it to a state-space model with <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="98219f4dc193c7e6f9a0384583eb8f69">$\approx 230\mathrm{k}$</math-renderer> dimensions in the context of spatiotemporal GP regression of climate/weather data with about <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="98219f4dc193c7e6f9a0384583eb8f69">$4$</math-renderer> million data points.
The code for the experiments from the paper can be found in <a href="https://github.com/marvinpfoertner/ComputationAwareKalmanExperiments.jl">ComputationAwareKalmanExperiments.jl</a>.</p>
