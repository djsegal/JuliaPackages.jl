<p dir="auto">The package implements <code>*</code>-algebras with basis. The prime example use are group
or monoid algebras (or their finite dimensional subspaces).
An example usage can be as follows.</p>

<p dir="auto">This package originated as a tool to compute sum of hermitian squares in
<code>*</code>-algebras. These consist not of standard <code>f*f</code> summands, but rather
<code>star(f)*f</code>. You may think of semi-definite matrices: their Cholesky
decomposition determines <code>P = Q'·Q</code>, where <code>Q'</code> denotes (conjugate) transpose.
Algebra of matrices with transpose is an (the?) example of a <code>*</code>-algebra.
To compute such sums of squares one may either sprinkle the code with <code>star</code>s,
or <code>'</code> (aka <code>Base.adjoint</code> postfix symbol):</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="julia&gt; x = RG(Permutation(perm&quot;(1,2,3)&quot;, G))

<p dir="auto"><code>b = SA.DiracBasis{UInt32}(G)</code> takes an iterator (in this case a finite
permutation group <code>G</code>) and creates a basis with Dirac multiplicative structure.
This means a basis of a linear space with the multiplicative structure where
multiplication of two basis elements results in a third one. This computation
does not depend on the finiteness of <code>G</code>, i.e. is fully lazy.</p>
<p dir="auto">When the basis is known a priori one can create an efficient <code>SA.FixedBasis</code>
which caches (memoizes) the results of multiplications. For example:</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="julia&gt; fb = let l = length(basis(RG))
