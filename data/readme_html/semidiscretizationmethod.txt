<p dir="auto">Julia package to investigate the behaviour of linear delay differential equations based on the book
<a href="http://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-1-4614-0335-7" rel="nofollow">Semi-Discretization for Time-Delay Systems (by Insperger and Stepan)</a>.</p>
<p dir="auto">This package provides a tool to approximate the stability properties and stationary behaviour of linear periodic delay systems of the form:</p>
<p dir="auto">$$ \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}(t) \mathbf{x}(t) + \sum_{j=1}^g \mathbf{B}<em>j(t) \mathbf{x}(t-\tau_j(t))+\mathbf{c}(t)$$
by transforming the underlying differential equation into the mapping:
$$\mathbf{y}</em>{n+1} = \mathbf{F}_n\mathbf{y}_n+\mathbf{f}_n,$$</p>
<p dir="auto">where <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$n$</math-renderer> is the discrete time (<math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$t_n = n \Delta t$</math-renderer>), <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$\mathbf{F}_n$</math-renderer> is the mapping matrix constructed using <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$\mathbf{A}(t)$</math-renderer>, <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$\mathbf{B}(t)$</math-renderer> and <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$\tau_j(t)$</math-renderer>, while the vector <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$\mathbf{y}_n$</math-renderer> is the discretized state space vector:</p>
<p dir="auto">$$ \mathbf{y}<em>n = \left(\mathbf{x}(t_n)^\top, \mathbf{x}(t</em>{n-1})^\top,\ldots,\mathbf{x}(t_{n-r})\right)^\top!.$$</p>
<p dir="auto">Each coefficient matrices of delay differential equations are periodic, with a principle period of <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$T$</math-renderer>, namely:
$\mathbf A(t)=\mathbf A(t+T),; \mathbf B_j(t)=\mathbf B_j(t+T),; \tau_j(t)=\tau_j(t+T)$) and <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$\mathbf{c}(t)=\mathbf{c}(t+T)$</math-renderer>
Furthermore, the integer <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$r$</math-renderer> is chosen in a way, that <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$r\Delta t\geq \max_{t \in \left[0,T\right],j=1\ldots g}\tau_j(t)$</math-renderer>
(the discretized "history function" contains all possible delayed values).</p>
<p dir="auto">With the use of the discrete (one-step) mappings the Monodromy matrix (or One-Period mapping) can be determied:
$$\mathbf{\Phi}<em>n =\prod</em>{i=0}^{p-1}\mathbf{F}_{n+i}$$</p>
<p dir="auto">The stability of the original system can be investigated (approximately), by the spectral radius <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$\rho$</math-renderer> of <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$\mathbf{\Phi}_n$</math-renderer>:</p>
<p dir="auto"><math-renderer class="js-display-math" style="display: block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f9891602e06996ba018c38120eac8446">$$\rho\left(\mathbf{\Phi}_n\right): \quad
\begin{matrix}
&lt;1 &amp; \Rightarrow &amp; \text{the mapping is stable}\\
&gt;1 &amp; \Rightarrow &amp; \text{the mapping is unstable}
\end{matrix}
$$</math-renderer></p>
<p dir="auto">Furthermore, the stationary solution can be determined by the periodic fix point (stationary orbit) of the mapping.</p>

<p dir="auto">It is found that the One-Period Mapping can be describen by two matrices:
$$\mathbf{\Phi _L}<em>n \mathbf{y}</em>{n+1} = \mathbf{\Phi _R}_n\mathbf{y}_n+\mathbf{v}_n,$$</p>
<p dir="auto">which can be created based on the one-step matrices. The quadratic time complexity is reduced to linear  with this modification.</p>
