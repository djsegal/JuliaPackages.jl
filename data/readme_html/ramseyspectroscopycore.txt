<p dir="auto">RamseySpectroscopyCore.jl provides core toolset for simulating Rabi/Ramsey spectroscopy experiments.
The main goal is to have ready-to-use, simple-to-understand modeling toolkit with experimenters in mind.
Furthermore, it can be used as introduction to spectroscopy for people with little physics background or
students.</p>

<p dir="auto">Lets start by creating our experement setup:</p>
<ol dir="auto">
<li>
<p dir="auto">Gas cell or a single atom - research object;</p>
</li>
<li>
<p dir="auto">Tunable laser - research instrument.</p>
</li>
</ol>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="julia&gt; using RabiRamseySpectroscopy
julia&gt; atom = StateVector(0, 1)
julia&gt; laser = PerturbEvol(0, 3.14, 1)"><pre>julia<span class="pl-k">&gt;</span> <span class="pl-k">using</span> RabiRamseySpectroscopy
julia<span class="pl-k">&gt;</span> atom <span class="pl-k">=</span> <span class="pl-c1">StateVector</span>(<span class="pl-c1">0</span>, <span class="pl-c1">1</span>)
julia<span class="pl-k">&gt;</span> laser <span class="pl-k">=</span> <span class="pl-c1">PerturbEvol</span>(<span class="pl-c1">0</span>, <span class="pl-c1">3.14</span>, <span class="pl-c1">1</span>)</pre></div>
<p dir="auto">Now we want to pump our atom with a laser to excited energy level:</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="julia&gt; pump!(atom, laser)"><pre>julia<span class="pl-k">&gt;</span> <span class="pl-c1">pump!</span>(atom, laser)</pre></div>
<p dir="auto">After that, atom needs to be probed with a second laser pulse to check a populaton of the excited
state (or how many atoms in a gas cell are excited).</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="julia&gt; probe(atom)
0.9999"><pre>julia<span class="pl-k">&gt;</span> <span class="pl-c1">probe</span>(atom)
<span class="pl-c1">0.9999</span></pre></div>
<p dir="auto">We'll get some value approximately close to 1 which means that laser frequency is resonant with
transition frequency of an atom.</p>
<p dir="auto">Congrats, you performed your first spectroscopy experement in Julia!</p>
