<p dir="auto"><code>FlexiJoins.jl</code> is a fresh take on joining tabular or non-tabular datasets in Julia.</p>
<p dir="auto">From simple joins by key, to asof joins, to merging catalogs of terrestrial or celestial coordinates – <code>FlexiJoins</code> supports any usecase.</p>
<p dir="auto">Defining features of FlexiJoins that make it <em>flexible</em>:</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>Wide range of join conditions:
<ul dir="auto">
<li>by key, so-called equi-join</li>
<li>by distance</li>
<li>by a comparison predicate: one of <code>&lt;, &lt;=, ==, &gt;=, &gt;</code></li>
<li>all matches or only the closest match</li>
<li>by an interval predicate: one of <code>∈, ⊆, ⊊, ⊋, ⊇, !isdisjoint</code></li>
<li>combinations of the above</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>All kinds of joins, as in inner/left/right/outer</li>
<li>Results can either be a flat list, or grouped by the left/right side</li>
<li>Lots of dataset types transparently supported: various arrays, dictionaries, tables</li>
<li>And more! See <a href="https://aplavin.github.io/FlexiJoins.jl/test/examples.html" rel="nofollow">examples</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p dir="auto">With all these features, FlexiJoins is designed to be easy-to-use and fast:</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>Uniform interface to all functionaly</li>
<li>Performance close to other, less general, solutions: see <a href="https://aplavin.github.io/FlexiJoins.jl/test/benchmarks.html" rel="nofollow">benchmarks</a> comparing with <code>SplitApplyCombine.jl</code> and <code>DataFrames.jl</code></li>
<li>Extensible in terms of both new join conditions and more specialized algorithms</li>
</ul>
