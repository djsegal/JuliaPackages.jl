<p dir="auto">PhyloNetworks is a <a href="http://julialang.org" rel="nofollow">Julia</a> package with utilities to:</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>read / write phylogenetic trees and networks,
in (extended) Newick format.
Networks are considered explicit: nodes represent ancestral species.
They can be rooted or unrooted.</li>
<li>manipulate networks: re-root, prune taxa, remove hybrid edges,
extract the major tree from a network, extract displayed networks / trees</li>
<li>compare networks / trees with dissimilarity measures
(Robinson-Foulds distance on trees)</li>
<li>summarize samples of bootstrap networks (or trees)
with edge and node support</li>
<li>estimate species networks from multilocus data: SNaQ</li>
<li>phylogenetic comparative methods for continuous trait evolution
on species networks / trees</li>
<li>plot networks (and trees), via the companion package
<a href="https://github.com/juliaphylo/PhyloPlots.jl">PhyloPlots</a></li>
</ul>
<p dir="auto">To get help, check</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>the <a href="https://juliaphylo.github.io/PhyloNetworks.jl/dev" rel="nofollow">latest documentation</a></li>
<li>the <a href="https://github.com/juliaphylo/PhyloNetworks.jl/wiki">wiki</a> for a step-by-step tutorial
with background on networks (last revised 2022)</li>
<li><a href="https://juliaphylo.github.io/networkPCM-workshop/" rel="nofollow">tutorial</a> for
comparative methods, including network calibration (2023 workshop)</li>

for common questions. Join the group to post/email your questions,
or to receive information on new versions, bugs fixed, etc.</li>
</ul>

<p dir="auto">SNaQ implements the statistical inference method in Solís-Lemus and Ané
<a href="http://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1005896" rel="nofollow">(2016)</a>.
The procedure involves a
numerical optimization of branch lengths and inheritance probabilities
and a heuristic search in the space of phylogenetic
networks.</p>
<p dir="auto">If you use SNaQ, please cite</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>Claudia Solís-Lemus and Cécile Ané (2016).
Inferring Phylogenetic Networks with Maximum Pseudolikelihood under Incomplete Lineage Sorting.
<a href="http://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1005896" rel="nofollow">PLoS Genet</a>
12(3):e1005896.
<a href="https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1005896" rel="nofollow">doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.1005896</a></li>
</ul>

<p dir="auto">For continuous traits, study based on the Brownian motion process,
with or without transgressive evolution after reticulations:</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>Bastide, Solís-Lemus, Kriebel, Sparks, Ané (2018).
Phylogenetic Comparative Methods for Phylogenetic Networks with Reticulations.
Systematic Biology, 67(5):800–820.
<a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syy033" rel="nofollow">doi:10.1093/sysbio/syy033</a>.
SI on <a href="http://dx.doi.org/10.5061/dryad.nt2g6" rel="nofollow">dryad</a>
including a tutorial for trait evolution
and a tutorial for network calibration.</li>
</ul>
<p dir="auto">Continuous traits, accounting for within-species variation:</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>Benjamin Teo, Jeffrey P. Rose, Paul Bastide &amp; Cécile Ané (2022).
Accounting for intraspecific variation in continuous trait evolution
on a reticulate phylogeny.
<a href="https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.05.12.490814" rel="nofollow">bioRxiv</a></li>
</ul>
<p dir="auto">For a discrete trait (influence of gene flow on the trait,
ancestral state reconstruction, rates):</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>Karimi, Grover, Gallagher, Wendel, Ané &amp; Baum (2020). Reticulate evolution
helps explain apparent homoplasy in floral biology and pollination in baobabs
(<em>Adansonia</em>; Bombacoideae; Malvaceae).
Systematic Biology,
69(3):462-478. doi: <a href="https://academic.oup.com/sysbio/advance-article/doi/10.1093/sysbio/syz073/5613901?guestAccessKey=a32e7dd3-27fd-4a13-b171-7ff5d6da0e01" rel="nofollow">10.1093/sysbio/syz073</a>.</li>
</ul>
