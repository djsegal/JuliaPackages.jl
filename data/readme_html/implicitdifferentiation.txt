<p dir="auto"><a href="https://github.com/JuliaDecisionFocusedLearning/ImplicitDifferentiation.jl">ImplicitDifferentiation.jl</a> is a package for automatic differentiation of functions defined implicitly, i.e., <em>forward mappings</em></p>
<math-renderer class="js-display-math" style="display: block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="ee9ffaced828046bbf427fe8944c93b2">$$x \in \mathbb{R}^n \longmapsto y(x) \in \mathbb{R}^m$$</math-renderer>
<p dir="auto">whose output is defined by <em>conditions</em></p>
<math-renderer class="js-display-math" style="display: block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="ee9ffaced828046bbf427fe8944c93b2">$$c(x,y(x)) = 0 \in \mathbb{R}^m$$</math-renderer>

<p dir="auto">Implicit differentiation is useful to differentiate through two types of functions:</p>
<ul dir="auto">
<li>Those for which automatic differentiation fails. Reasons can vary depending on your backend, but the most common include calls to external solvers, mutating operations or type restrictions.</li>
<li>Those for which automatic differentiation is very slow. A common example is iterative procedures like fixed point equations or optimization algorithms.</li>
</ul>
<p dir="auto">If you just need a quick overview, check out our <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TkVDcujVNJ4&amp;feature=youtu.be" rel="nofollow">JuliaCon 2022 talk</a>.
If you want a deeper dive into the theory, you can refer to the paper <a href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/228b9279ecf9bbafe582406850c57115-Abstract-Conference.html" rel="nofollow"><em>Efficient and modular implicit differentiation</em></a> by Blondel et al. (2022).</p>
