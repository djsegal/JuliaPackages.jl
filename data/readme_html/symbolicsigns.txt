<p dir="auto">This package provides types to work with symbolic signs that often come up
in differential homological algebra and other graded contexts. Specifically,
given any commutative ring <code>R</code> and any type <code>T</code>, the package allows to define
a new commutative ring <code>SymbolicSignRing{T,R}</code> that extends <code>R</code> by expressions
of the form <code>(-1)^|x|</code> and <code>(-1)^(|x|*|y|)</code> where <code>|x|</code> and <code>|y|</code> represent
the symbolic degrees of <code>x::T</code> and <code>y::T</code>, respectively.</p>
<p dir="auto">The symbolic signs <code>(-1)^(|x|*|y|)</code> and <code>(-1)^(|y|*|x|)</code> are treated as equal,
and <code>(-1)^(|x|*|x|)</code> is simplified to <code>(-1)^|x|</code>.</p>

<p dir="auto">Signs usually come up whenever two graded objects are swapped. Here is an example
with tensors, based on the package <a href="https://github.com/matthias314/LinearCombinations.jl">LinearCombinations.jl</a>.
We define the degree of a small letter to be its position in the alphabet.
Then we swap the factors of a
<a href="https://matthias314.github.io/LinearCombinations.jl/stable/tensor/#LinearCombinations.Tensor" rel="nofollow"><code>Tensor</code></a>
with the function
<a href="https://matthias314.github.io/LinearCombinations.jl/stable/tensor/#LinearCombinations.swap" rel="nofollow"><code>swap</code></a>.
This incurs a sign which is the product of the degrees of the factors.</p>

<p dir="auto">We now do the same with symbolic signs. The computation of the sign out of the degrees
is done under the hood here, but it illustrates the functionality of the package.
Below we will construct signs explicitly.</p>
