<p dir="auto">One of the big triumphs in liquid state theory is the ability to approximate the structure of a liquid from the way its constituent particles interact.
This can be done using the exact Ornstein-Zernike equation:
<math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f2357793595a65a89f66d8146c1c4d9a">$$h(r) = c(r) + \rho \int d\textbf{r}' c(\textbf{r}')h(\textbf{r}- \textbf{r}') $$</math-renderer></p>
<p dir="auto">Here <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f2357793595a65a89f66d8146c1c4d9a">$h(r) = g(r) - 1$</math-renderer>, where <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f2357793595a65a89f66d8146c1c4d9a">$g(r)$</math-renderer> is the radial distribution function, which describes the structure of a liquid, <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f2357793595a65a89f66d8146c1c4d9a">$\rho$</math-renderer> is the number density, and <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f2357793595a65a89f66d8146c1c4d9a">$c(r)$</math-renderer> is the direct correlation function. Together with an approximate closure relation, which links <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f2357793595a65a89f66d8146c1c4d9a">$c(r)$</math-renderer> to the interaction potential of the particles, this integral equation can be solved.</p>
<p dir="auto">This package implements common solution methods for the Ornstein Zernike equation, for single component systems as well as mixtures, in any dimension. It implements many predefined closure relations and many interaction potentials. Moreover, it let's you easily use your own closures and interaction potentials.</p>

<p dir="auto">Let's solve the Ornstein-Zernike equation for a single component three-dimensional system of hard spheres at number density <math-renderer class="js-inline-math" style="display: inline-block" data-static-url="https://github.githubassets.com/static" data-run-id="f2357793595a65a89f66d8146c1c4d9a">$ρ = 0.5$</math-renderer>.</p>
<div class="highlight highlight-source-julia notranslate position-relative overflow-auto" dir="auto" data-snippet-clipboard-copy-content="using OrnsteinZernike

potential = HardSpheres(1.0)

closure = PercusYevick()
sol = @time solve(system, closure);"><pre><span class="pl-k">using</span> OrnsteinZernike
